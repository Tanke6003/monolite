# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people can disagree with.

This project argues about design in public and on purpose — the packages are
full of comments explaining why a decision went one way, and the issue templates
ask what alternative was rejected. That only works if the argument stays about
the work.

## What that means here

**Criticise the design, not the person who proposed it.** "This breaks on MySQL
because its default collation is case-insensitive" is the whole job. "You
clearly did not test this" is the same information with a person attached to it,
and it makes the next report quieter than it should be.

**Assume the other person met a case you have not.** Most decisions that look
wrong were made against a constraint that is not visible from where you are
standing. Ask what it was before explaining why they are mistaken.

**Say when you are unsure.** A maintainer who never says "I do not know why this
happens on Oracle" produces a repository where nobody else can either.

**English or Spanish, either is fine.** The documentation exists in both and so
does the maintainer. Write in whichever you think more clearly in — an issue in
imperfect English is worth more than one that never got opened.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Demeaning comments about someone's
identity, background, or level of experience. Unwanted attention after being
asked to stop. Publishing someone's private information. Deliberately derailing
a thread that other people are trying to use.

Beginners get the same answer as everyone else, at more length. A question that
is answered in the documentation is a question the documentation failed to
answer where the person was looking.

## Scope

The issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere someone is
representing this project.

## Enforcement

Report it privately to **ruben.farias.1999@outlook.es**, or through GitHub's
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) if the person to report
is the maintainer.

What happens depends on what happened: a comment edited and a word in private, a
thread locked, or someone blocked from the repository. There is one maintainer
here, which means enforcement is neither anonymous nor a committee — reports are
read by the person who acts on them, and kept between the two of you.

Anyone asked to stop something is expected to stop immediately. Continuing after
that is its own answer.
